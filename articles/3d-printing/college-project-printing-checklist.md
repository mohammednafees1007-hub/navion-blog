# 3D Printing for College Projects: A Submission Checklist

A college project does not end when the parts arrive. You may still need to assemble the model, route wires, test the demonstration and prepare your explanation. Plan the printing request around those remaining tasks.

## Decide what the printed part must demonstrate

Write one sentence describing the purpose of each part. Is it a visual model, a cover, a mounting piece or part of a moving assembly? Mark which features are necessary for the demonstration and which are optional details. This gives the print reviewer a clearer picture of your priorities.

For an electronics project, include the dimensions of the actual board, switches and connectors you intend to use. A generic product picture may not match the hardware you have purchased. Tell the reviewer whether those components are already available for measurement.

## Make a parts list before uploading

Give every file a descriptive name and list the number of copies needed. Keep the approved version separate from earlier experiments. A small project might have a base, a lid and two brackets; sending four files without quantities leaves room for confusion.

Record the dimensions that matter for assembly. If a fit is uncertain, ask whether a small sample or a first version is practical before committing to the full set. Sample printing, redesign and further iterations need their own time and quotation.

## Work backwards from the presentation

Reserve time for assembly and checking after delivery. Give NAVION 3D the date you need the parts in hand, not only the date of the examination. Ask for confirmation of the proposed schedule; do not assume an urgent request has been accepted.

For your report, distinguish the model you designed, the part that was printed and the results you personally tested. A successful print is not proof that the complete project works.
