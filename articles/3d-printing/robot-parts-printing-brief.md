# Planning 3D-Printed Parts for a Student Robot

A student robot brings mechanical parts, electronics and software together. Before requesting a printed chassis or mount, gather the actual components you plan to assemble. A change in motor, sensor board or battery holder can change the dimensions your design needs.

## List the hardware first

Record the motors, wheels, controller board, sensor modules and fasteners. Include measured dimensions or the exact manufacturer's drawings where available. Mark mounting holes, connector locations and any moving parts that need clearance.

Explain how you plan to install and remove components. For example, a controller may fit inside a cover while its USB connector remains inaccessible. Describe the access you need for programming, charging and routine adjustments.

## Identify the purpose of each print

A chassis, sensor bracket and cable guide have different jobs. Name each part and explain how it connects to the others. If the design allows adjustments, show the desired positions and how you intend to secure them.

When a dimension is uncertain, discuss a small fit-check part before a full assembly. Treat this as a separate step in the budget and timeline. A sample can answer a specific fit question; it does not establish that the complete robot will drive or sense correctly.

## Keep the first test measurable

Prepare a short assembly checklist: holes line up, connectors are accessible, wheels can move freely and the sensor can be placed as intended. Record what you actually observe and revise the model accordingly.

These are planning examples, not claims that NAVION has tested a particular robot design. Printing requests are reviewed individually, and feasibility depends on the submitted model and requirements.
