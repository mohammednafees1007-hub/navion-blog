# Choosing a Print Material Starts with the Job

Choosing a material is easier when you first explain the job. A familiar material name does not tell the reviewer where the part will sit, how it will be handled or what it must connect to.

NAVION 3D lists PLA, PLA+, PETG and TPU as materials to discuss for project printing. Availability, colour and suitability are confirmed for the particular request. The list is a starting point for a conversation, not a guarantee that every option suits every model.

## Describe the environment

Say whether the part will stay on a desk, travel inside a bag or be used outdoors. Mention exposure to heat, sunlight, moisture or cleaning products when relevant. If there is a known temperature or other operating requirement, provide it rather than using a broad description such as “must be strong.”

## Describe the movement and load

Will the part remain still, bend, be squeezed, or support another component? Explain what it carries and how it is attached. A sketch showing where a force acts can communicate more than a material preference alone.

For a noncritical prototype, define what you want to learn from the first print. You might be checking whether a shape fits your hand or whether a board fits inside an enclosure. Those questions can be discussed separately from the requirements of a finished working part.

## Ask for a project-specific recommendation

Send your model and requirements, then ask which available material is suitable and why. Confirm any limits before approving the job. Do not treat a decorative sample or a successful fit check as validation of long-term performance.

A useful request reads: “This part holds a small display on an indoor desk. It should remain rigid, and these mounting dimensions matter. Please suggest a suitable available option.”
