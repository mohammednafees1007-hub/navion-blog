# How to Give Useful Feedback on a Companion Robot Concept

You do not need robotics knowledge to give useful feedback on a companion concept. A clear account of your own routine can help more than a long list of futuristic features.

NAVION ROBOTICS is gathering input during early research and concept development. The survey helps explore needs and priorities; completing it is not an order, and suggestions are not promises of future features.

## Describe a recent situation

Think of a time when you wanted help while studying, creating or working on a project. Explain what you were trying to do, where you were and what interrupted or slowed you down. Avoid including private details that are unnecessary to explain the situation.

Next, describe what you did instead. Did you set a reminder, ask someone, write a note or simply postpone the task? Existing habits show what a new concept would need to fit around.

## Describe the help, not just the technology

“I want AI and voice control” leaves many questions open. “I want a quiet way to remember my next task without interrupting people in the room” gives a clearer direction to investigate.

You can still suggest features, but connect them to the outcome you want. Explain whether you would prefer a spoken response, a visible signal or no interruption at that moment.

## Say what would make you reject it

Feedback about boundaries is valuable. Mention unwanted noise, space constraints, privacy concerns or interactions you would find distracting. If an existing app already solves the problem, say so. Research benefits from understanding when a companion would not be useful.

A concise response can follow four prompts: my situation, my current workaround, the help I would welcome and the limits I would want respected.
