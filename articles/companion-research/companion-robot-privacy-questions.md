# Privacy Questions to Ask About a Desk Companion Robot

A device that shares a desk may also share a room with classmates, family members or colleagues. Privacy questions should therefore include both the person using it and the people nearby.

NAVION ROBOTICS is in the research stage of a companion robot project. This article describes questions to explore; it does not claim that a particular sensor, privacy control or data-processing architecture has been selected or implemented.

## What would the device need to sense?

Begin with the intended task. If the goal is a simple reminder, what information is actually needed to deliver it? If voice interaction is proposed, how would the user know when the device is listening? If a camera is considered, what purpose would justify its use?

These are design questions to investigate rather than assumptions that a future product must contain every possible sensor.

## What should the user be able to control?

Research can ask whether people prefer a visible listening indicator, a physical control, a clear pause state or another way to understand the device's status. A control also needs to be understandable: a person should not have to guess what happens when they press it.

Questions about stored information matter too. What would be saved, for what purpose, for how long and how could it be removed? Those details require explicit decisions before any claims about a finished system would be appropriate.

## How does a shared room change the answer?

A voice prompt that is acceptable at home might feel intrusive in a library. A visitor may not know what a nearby device is doing. Ask how the concept should communicate its status to people who are not its owner.

You can contribute without sharing sensitive examples. Describe your preferences and boundaries in general terms. [Visit NAVION ROBOTICS' research page](https://navionrobotics.com/) to find the current survey and its information notice.
